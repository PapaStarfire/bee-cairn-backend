# Psychagogue: The Canonical Definition

Source of truth for every use of this word across the practice. Lift from here rather than
rewriting, so the definition stays the same one everywhere it appears.

**Register.** The definition is **Canon Two, OmiGaia/SUPERGRIEF**. It is a framework document
and public-facing copy, which is the Canon Two venue list. A Hermification version is held at
the bottom of this file for the Herald and for nowhere else.

## Pronunciation

**SY-kuh-gog.** Like demagogue, with a psyche in front.
The practice is **psychagogy**, said *sy-KAG-uh-jee*, in the same shape as pedagogy.

Give the pronunciation every time the word is introduced to someone new. A word a person
cannot say is a word that makes them feel stupid, and a grieving person has no reserve for that.

---

## The four rules

Every use of this word, in any length, must hold all four.

1. **Direction is stated early.** The oldest sense of the word describes someone who conducted
   souls into the underworld or called them back out. That reading is foreclosed inside the
   definition itself, in the same breath as the etymology that invites it. Never in a footer.
2. **The persuasion sense is rejected, not inherited.** Contemporary dictionaries define a
   psychagogue as a deeply persuasive figure who moves people through rhetorical skill.
   That is the opposite of the posture this practice holds. Say so plainly.
3. **The precursor claim is marked as interpretation.** That psychagogy is an ancestor of
   psychotherapy is a reading of the history, not a settled finding. Layer Two, never Layer One.
4. **The body is named as the new part.** Not listed third. The ancient term had no body in it,
   and modern psychotherapy largely surrendered the body as well. That is the original
   contribution and understating it wastes the only genuinely new element.

## The short definition

Use this one by default. It is the version built to be quoted whole.

> **Psychagogue** (SY-kuh-gog), from the Greek *psychē*, soul, and *agōgos*, one who leads.
>
> A psychagogue walks beside a person through what grief changes, at every level it reaches
> them: in the body, in the mind, and in whatever they hold sacred. Not a clinician treating a
> condition. Not a coach improving a performance. Not a guide with the route already drawn.
> Someone who carries a lamp, walks at your pace, and trusts your own interior knowledge
> further than their own.
>
> The word is older than psychology and it names a good deal of what psychology was built out
> of. Psychology kept the *psyche* and set the *agōgos* down. The leading was the part that
> got left behind.
>
> One thing it does not mean here. The oldest sense of this word described someone who
> conducted souls across into the underworld, or called them back out of it. This practice does
> neither. The psychagogue described here walks the living to the threshold and back again.
> Nobody is summoned. Nobody is contacted. What continues, continues without my assistance.

## The expanded definition

For the website, talks, framework documents, and anywhere the word gets room to stand up.

> **Psychagogue.** SY-kuh-gog. From the Greek *psychē*, soul, and *agōgos*, one who leads.
> Soul-leading. It is a very old word and it has been sitting in a drawer for roughly two
> thousand years.
>
> I use it because nothing else available is accurate. Counselor imports the clinic, and this
> work is not clinical. Coach imports performance, and there is nothing here to improve.
> Guide imports a route, and a route is the one thing I will not offer, because the person
> carrying this already knows terrain I have never walked. Psychagogue means walking beside
> someone through the country of their own soul without claiming to have mapped it. That is
> the actual job.
>
> **Where it comes from.** In Plato's *Phaedrus*, rhetoric is called *psychagōgia*, the leading
> of souls through speech. In the Greek and Roman philosophical schools that followed, the word
> settled around the care of the soul itself: the long, unhurried practice of bringing a life
> into alignment with what that life actually holds true. Scholars who read that tradition
> closely, Hadot and Foucault among them, describe it as lived practice rather than doctrine.
> Reading it as an ancestor of modern psychotherapy is an interpretation and I offer it as one.
> What is not in question is the shape: someone sat with someone else, over time, and attended
> to their soul, and that happened for centuries before anyone thought to call it treatment.
>
> **What I am not saying.** The oldest use of this word named a conductor of souls across into
> the underworld, and sometimes a necromancer who called them back out. I am not doing that and
> I want no ambiguity about it. I do not contact anyone who has crossed, I do not carry
> messages, and I do not claim access to anything on the other side of a threshold I have not
> crossed myself. What continues, continues without my assistance. I walk the living to the
> threshold and back again. That is the whole of the direction, and the direction is the
> difference.
>
> **What I am also not saying.** The word survives in modern usage to mean a persuasive person,
> someone whose rhetoric reaches soul-deep. I decline that meaning entirely. Persuasion is the
> opposite of this work. A psychagogue who persuades has stopped walking beside and started
> walking in front, and the person following has lost the one thing that was theirs.
>
> **Three levels, and the body first.** Grief is not only a thought about what changed. It
> arrives in the body before it reaches language, and it goes on living in whatever a person
> holds sacred long after the language runs out. So the work moves across all three.
>
> The body, because it is where this lands first and where it stays longest. The mind, because
> meaning has to be rebuilt and that is thinking work. And the spirit, whatever a person means
> by that word, including nothing, because for most people something in their architecture of
> meaning has shifted and nobody has helped them look at it.
>
> The body is the part that is genuinely new here. The ancient psychagogue had no somatic
> practice, and modern psychotherapy largely handed the body to medicine and walked away from
> it. Putting it back is not a flourish. It is the correction.

## Modality and framework

**Psychagogy is the modality. Grief Cosmology is the framework.** This distinction is
architectural and it is never collapsed.

| | Grief Cosmology | Psychagogy |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The framework | The modality |
| What it answers | What is true about what continues | How a person is walked through it |
| Where it lives | Position papers, framework documents, the three-layer epistemology | The hour, the practice, the room |
| What it holds | Conservation of energy, the Luminous Horizon, the Living Ground, the Light Bookend, the Five Sacred Geometries, the Peregrine identity | Walking beside, the lamp rather than the map, body and mind and spirit, the Deep Organ Sequence |

A framework with no modality is a belief. A modality with no framework is a technique.
Held together they are a practice.

**Rules that follow from the distinction.**

- Never describe psychagogy as a framework. It is a modality and describing it otherwise
  makes it sound like a worldview being sold rather than a method being practiced.
- Never describe Grief Cosmology as a modality. It is not something done to or with a person
  in an hour. It is the ground the hour stands on.
- Public copy names the framework and does not argue it. The three-layer epistemology needs
  room to keep Layer One separate from Layer Three, and marketing copy cannot hold that
  separation. Name it, point elsewhere, move on.
- The hero headline is downstream of the framework. "Honoring What Continues" is a
  conservation claim. That is the framework doing its job in three words without naming itself.

## Three levels and the Deep Organ Sequence

The three levels are not an assertion. They rest on the canonical sequence, which is why the
order is what it is.

| Level | Organ | The question |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Gut | Are brain and heart aligned? |
| Mental | Brain | What do I think? |
| Spiritual | Heart | How do I feel about what I think? |

The sequence runs Brain, Heart, Gut and is never reversed. Brain-Heart alignment is a
biological prerequisite for gut coherence, because sympathetic activation halts digestion,
and roughly eighty percent of vagal traffic runs upward from gut to brain. The Deep Organ
Sequence creates the conditions for those transmissions to be received.

Where DOT names the order of the work, the three levels name the territory the work covers.
Use DOT in session and in practitioner material. Use the three levels in public copy.
Do not mix the two vocabularies in one paragraph.

## Never

- Never define the word without giving the pronunciation.
- Never lead a page, a talk, or an introduction with the word. Plain sentence first, word second.
- Never imply contact with anyone who has crossed. Not as a hint, not as an ambiguity,
  not as something left conveniently open.
- Never inherit the persuasion sense.
- Never state the precursor claim as settled fact.
- Never pair it with "Dr."
- Never use it as the only descriptor in a bio. It carries identity; it cannot carry discovery.
