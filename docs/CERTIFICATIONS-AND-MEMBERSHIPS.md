# Scope Expansion: Certifications and Memberships

Assessment of low and no cost credentials and affiliations that widen what this
practice can legitimately claim, hold, and be referred into.

Prices were checked in September 2026 and are recorded with their source. Prices move.
Confirm on the provider's own page before paying anything. Where a figure could not be
verified it is marked **unverified** rather than estimated.

---

## The gap, named plainly

The standing already held is ordination, plus ICGC, plus the specializations.
That covers authority and it covers method. Three gaps sit underneath it.

**Gap one is risk recognition.** This is an international, non-clinical practice working with
people at a threshold. Someone at risk will arrive. Right now there is no named, dated,
third-party credential on file showing formal training in recognizing that and referring
it onward. This is the largest exposure in the whole practice and it is also the cheapest
thing on this page to close.

**Gap two is the dream affiliation.** The site states that the practice is aligned with IASD
Ethical Standards. That statement is accurate and defensible as written. Membership would
make it unambiguous, and it is the single most on-brand affiliation available.

**Gap three is accrued hours.** Every serious thanatology credential gates on documented
hours. Hours are not bought. They are accumulated, and accumulation has to start before
the credential is wanted, not when it is wanted.

Everything below is sequenced against those three.

---

## Tier 0: Free. Start inside thirty days.

### Hospice volunteer training, through a local Medicare-certified hospice
**Cost: none. Often the strongest single move on this page.**

Medicare-certified hospices are required to use volunteers and they train those volunteers
at no charge. The training is secular and interfaith by regulatory necessity, because the
hospice serves every patient regardless of tradition. That makes it a far better fit here
than the free chaplaincy programs that turn up first in a search, which are explicitly
Christian and biblically framed and would sit badly against an interfaith, Earth-centered,
Jewish-universalist practice.

What it returns, beyond the training itself:

- Supervised bedside hours, documented by an institution, in a field that counts.
- An institutional reference that is not self-issued.
- A referral relationship with the hospice's own bereavement coordinator, who fields
  requests constantly for exactly this kind of non-clinical accompaniment.
- The hours that ADEC certification later requires. See Tier 3.

The free thing feeds the expensive thing. That is the strategic shape of this whole plan.

### Trauma-informed foundations, audited
**Cost: none to audit. Certificate optional.**

Coursera and edX both allow full course access without payment. Coursera additionally runs
a financial aid process that can bring a paid certificate to zero, applied for per course,
with a waiting period of roughly two weeks. Johns Hopkins publishes a Psychological First
Aid course on Coursera built on the RAPID model, free to take, with payment required only
for the certificate. OpenWHO carries a parallel Psychological First Aid course on the same
model, also free to take.

Take the content free. Apply for financial aid on the one certificate worth having on paper.

### Death Cafe facilitation
**Cost: none.**

Hosting is free and the model is open. It is not a credential and should never be presented
as one. What it is: a public-facing, entirely secular, internationally recognized format that
puts a practitioner in front of people who are already thinking about the threshold, at zero
cost, with no gatekeeper. As a referral source for an online practice it outperforms most
paid advertising.

## Tier 1: Under one hundred and fifty dollars. Highest return per dollar on this page.

### QPR Gatekeeper certification
**Cost: $29.95. About one hour. Certificate valid three years.**
Source: QPR Institute individual training page, checked September 2026.

This closes gap one. One hour, thirty dollars, a dated certificate from a named third party
listed in SAMHSA's registry. For a non-clinical practitioner meeting grieving people
internationally, this is the least defensible thing to not have.

Check local coalitions first. Many regional mental health organizations hold grant funding
that provides QPR at no cost to community members. The certificate is identical.

### NEDA proficiency badge, National End-of-Life Doula Alliance
**Cost: membership around $50, assessment $115. One full scholarship offered monthly.**
Source: NEDA credentials and membership pages, checked September 2026. Membership tier
pricing was not fully confirmable and should be checked directly.

Membership is a prerequisite for the assessment. The badge is competence-based rather than
training-brand-based, which means existing experience can be brought to it instead of
paying a training provider several thousand dollars first. That structure is unusual and it
is what makes this worth doing.

It also widens scope in a direction the current offering does not yet reach: the time before
the crossing, not only after it. Families looking for a doula are looking earlier, and they
keep the relationship afterward.

Apply for the monthly scholarship before paying the assessment fee.

## Tier 2: Worth buying once revenue is steady.

### IASD membership
**Cost: unverified.** The IASD site could not be reached from this session, so no figure is
recorded here rather than guessing at one. Check asdreams.org directly.

Closes gap two. Aligns a stated ethical position with a held affiliation, gives access to the
association's own conference and publications, and places the practice inside the one
professional body whose ethical framing is already quoted on the website. Of every affiliation
on this page, this is the one most precisely matched to what is already being offered.

### Somatic training, chosen carefully
The somatic field runs expensive and the large programs run into five figures across several
years. Nothing in Tier 2 should touch those. What is worth paying for at this tier is
short-form polyvagal-informed coursework that produces a certificate of completion and a
defensible citation, without implying clinical scope that is not held.

The framing rule stays the same regardless of what is bought: these are regulation practices
offered pastorally. They are not treatment, and no certificate purchased at this tier changes
that sentence.

## Tier 3: The ceiling raiser. Gated, and the gate is the point.

### ADEC Certified in Thanatology (CT)
**Renewal: $220 for members, $439 for non-members, every three years, with 45 contact hours.
Initial exam fee not confirmed.**
Source: ADEC certification pages and third-party credential directories, checked September 2026.

This is the credential that would most raise the practice's ceiling. It is the recognized
thanatology certification in the field and it carries weight with hospices, funeral
professionals, and institutional referrers in a way that a coaching certification does not.

The eligibility gate is real and must be checked before any money moves:

- A bachelor's degree or higher in health science, behavioral or social science, human
  services, or a related field.
- One year or more of experience in the field, counted as 1,760 hours.

Two honest observations. First, if the degree requirement is not met, this is not a near-term
target and no amount of preparation changes that. Second, the 1,760 hours are the reason
Tier 0 hospice volunteering sits at the top of this page. Starting those hours now costs
nothing and makes this credential reachable later. Not starting them keeps it permanently
out of reach regardless of budget.

Check the degree gate first. Everything else about this item is downstream of that one answer.

## What not to buy

The grief coaching certification market is full of programs charging between five hundred and
several thousand dollars for credentials that confer no recognized standing, appear in no
registry, and are not known to any referrer. With ICGC already held, a second certification
of that kind adds letters and nothing else.

The filter is three questions. Does a third party outside the issuing organization recognize
this. Would a hospice bereavement coordinator know the initials. Does it appear in any public
registry. Anything that fails all three is a purchase of letters, and there are already enough
letters on the credentials bar. The Shadow's warning from the council applies to credentials
exactly as it applied to the website copy: past a certain point, more initials read as less
substance, not more.

## Sequence

**Months one and two.** Contact two local hospices about volunteer training. Complete QPR,
after checking whether a local coalition provides it at no cost. Confirm whether the ADEC
degree gate is met, because that single answer shapes years three and four.

**Months three to six.** Begin hospice hours and start a simple log from the first shift,
dated, with supervisor name and hours. Audit the Psychological First Aid coursework. Apply for
financial aid on the one certificate worth holding. Host a first Death Cafe.

**Months six to twelve.** Join NEDA, apply for the monthly scholarship, sit the proficiency
assessment. Join IASD once the fee is confirmed and revenue supports it.

**Year two onward.** Continue accruing documented hours. Revisit ADEC only if the degree gate
is met. If it is not, the honest answer is that the ceiling is set by ordination and ICGC plus
lived specialization, which is a sound place to stand and does not need apologizing for.
