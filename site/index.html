Plain terms, before anything else

Knowing the shape of the container matters more than any promise about what it holds. Here is the shape.

What this is

  • Ecclesiastical pastoral care and spiritual counsel.
  • Non-clinical grief coaching and accompaniment.
  • Somatic regulation practice you keep and use on your own.
  • Dreamwork held under IASD ethical framing, where the meaning stays yours.
  • Open to secular, humanist, interfaith, deconstructing, and spiritual-but-not-religious people equally.

What this is not

  • Licensed clinical psychotherapy.
  • Psychiatric care, diagnosis, or prescription.
  • Crisis or emergency service.
  • A substitute for medical or clinical mental health treatment.
  • A tradition being sold to you, or a belief you are asked to hold.

If clinical care is what serves you right now, that is worth saying out loud, and I will say it out loud. Many people carry both at once. Pastoral accompaniment sits alongside clinical treatment comfortably. It does not replace it.

How I work

A lamp, not a map

I walk alongside rather than ahead. There is no sequence of stages here, no itinerary, and no correct pace. The lamp shows the ground directly underfoot. Where you step is yours to decide.

The body is not a symptom

A chest that will not open, sleep that will not come, a stomach that turns at the sound of a name. These are a nervous system doing exactly what it was built to do. We work with it rather than against it, using simple regulation practices you can carry into an ordinary Tuesday.

Your meaning stays yours

I never tell you what a dream means. I offer what an image might hold if it were mine, in the established "if this were my dream" framing, and you keep every part of it you recognize. You are the authority on your own interior.

About

As an Ordained Interfaith Clergy Person, Terran Judaism Rabbi, and Internationally Certified Grief Coach, I keep a non-clinical sanctuary for people standing at a threshold. I work with somatic nervous system regulation, symbolic dream exploration, and Earth-centered spiritual care. Clients come from across the world to sit with visitation dreams, to honor continuing bonds, and to let the body settle enough that meaning can return on its own terms.

My ordination is interfaith and my practice is deliberately non-doctrinal. I hold Earth-centered, Humanist, and Jewish-universalist traditions together with evidence-informed somatic principles, and I do not require you to hold any of them. People arrive here secular, devout, uncertain, furious at a tradition that raised them, or quietly rebuilding something they cannot name yet. All of that is ordinary ground. None of it needs resolving before we begin.

The word I use for this posture is psychagogue, from the Greek for soul and guide. It describes someone who walks beside rather than treats. It is the oldest description I have found of what actually happens in the room, and it is honest about its own limits.

Services and modalities

Three strands. Most people use all three inside a single hour without planning to. You are welcome to ask for one and leave the others alone.

01

Pastoral Grief Counseling and Accompaniment

Unhurried, confidential companionship for the terrain you are actually standing on. We speak about the person or the life that changed, about what continues, about the rituals that went missing and the ones you might want to build. Anniversaries, milestones, empty chairs at tables, the second year that nobody warned you would be harder than the first. Spiritual language is available if you want it and entirely optional if you do not.

Often chosen for: a recent crossing, a long ache with no container, faith that shifted or fell away, grief nobody around you recognizes as grief.

02

Somatic Nervous System Regulation

Grief lives in the body before it reaches language. We work with breath, orientation, weight, sound, and the felt sense of the ground, so that the body has somewhere to put what it is carrying. Nothing here is a cure and nothing is performance. These are small, repeatable practices, built to survive a bad night and a bad connection.

Often chosen for: sleeplessness, a chest that will not release, appetite that vanished, the shakes, numbness, the specific exhaustion of holding yourself together in public.

03

Symbolic and Visitation Dreamwork

Dreams are treated here as wisdom-bearing, not as symptoms to be corrected. Visitation dreams, recurring images, the dream that woke you at four in the morning and would not leave. We stay inside IASD ethical framing throughout: I speak only in the form of "if this were my dream," you take whatever resonates, and you leave the rest without explanation or apology. No interpretation is ever imposed on you.

Often chosen for: visitation dreams, dreams that repeat, dream imagery around a threshold, the wish to keep a dream rather than analyze it away.

Within this practice

Guy-Wire Counseling

Threshold work for men.

A guy-wire is the stabilizing cable that keeps a mast standing. It does not make the mast rigid. It anchors the mast to the ground so it can move in wind and still be there in the morning. And a guy-wire is never installed alone. They work in threes. No single cable holds a tower up.

Men are usually handed a job instead of a container. Be steady for everyone else. Handle the arrangements. Answer the questions. Be back at work on Monday. Nobody says any of it out loud and it gets done anyway. What the body is carrying does not evaporate because there was no room made for it. It goes underground. It comes back up sideways, months later, as a temper, a numbness, a drink, a silence at the dinner table that nobody can name.

Guy-Wire is the room built for the part that went underground.

The work is the same work described above. Pastoral accompaniment. Somatic regulation. Dreamwork held under IASD framing. What changes is the room. Nothing needs explaining from the beginning. Nobody is asked to perform composure. A man who has not said any of this out loud in eleven months can start in the middle of a sentence and be understood.

The room is built for men. Anyone who was handed that same silence is welcome in it.

What an hour holds

The most common question, and the one most practice sites leave unanswered. A sixty minute session usually moves through four movements. It is a shape, not a schedule. Any part can expand and any part can be skipped entirely.

  1. 5 min

    Arriving in the body

    A short somatic grounding so that you are actually here and not still in the meeting you just left. Eyes open or closed. Camera on or off. Entirely your call.

  2. 20 min

    Pastoral listening

    You speak. I listen without steering, without diagnosing, and without moving you toward a conclusion. Silence is allowed to be long. Nothing needs to be interesting.

  3. 20 min

    Dream and symbol exploration

    If you brought a dream or an image, we sit with it inside the "if this were my dream" frame. If you did not, this time goes wherever it is needed instead.

  4. 10 min

    Integration and what you carry out

    A closing regulation practice, and one small thing you choose to take with you. Chosen by you, never assigned by me. Some people leave with a practice. Some leave with a sentence. Some leave with nothing, and that is a complete session.

Before your first session it helps to have a quiet place you will not be interrupted, headphones if you have them, water within reach, and something to write on if you want to keep anything. None of it is required.

Intake and disclaimers

The intake form is completed once, before a first session. It takes most people under ten minutes. It carries the consent language that makes the non-clinical boundary explicit, and it asks for a local emergency contact, because this practice is international and I cannot reach emergency services in your country.

What it collects

Contact details, time zone, a local emergency contact, and the grief and spiritual context you choose to share.

Where it lives

In the form provider's encrypted storage and in my own records. It is a pastoral record, not a medical record, and it is never sold or shared.

What you sign

An electronic acknowledgement that these services are non-clinical pastoral care and grief coaching, and are not treatment.
